Most organizations were not designed. They accumulated, one reorganization, acquisition and new hire at a time. The result is familiar: too many layers, spans of three or four, overlapping roles, and decisions that climb to the executive team because nobody below is sure they are allowed to make them.

We redesign organizations from the work outward. We start with the outcomes the strategy requires and the decisions that drive them, then design roles, structures and governance around those. Only then do we look at the people who will fill the roles.

That order matters. It keeps the design honest, it makes the case for change easier to explain, and it gives leaders a structure they can hold to when the next reorganization is proposed. We stay through appointments and the first months of operation, because that is where most redesigns lose their shape.
